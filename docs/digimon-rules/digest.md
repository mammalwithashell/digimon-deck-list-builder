## 0. Fundamental principles (§1-2, §1-3) — internalize these first

- **Victory:** you win by a successful attack on the opponent while they have **0 security** with a Digimon that can perform ≥1 security check (1-2-3-1, 11-5-1-2-1); or by **deck-out** — opponent can't draw in their draw phase (1-2-3-2). Forfeit is an immediate loss and **does not trigger/activate effects** (1-2-4, 1-2-5).
- **Card text beats rules.** Any card text that conflicts with the rules takes priority (1-3-1).
- **Impossible / no-op actions:** an impossible action is skipped; do as many as possible (1-3-2). Changing to a state already held does nothing (1-3-3). Processing 0-or-fewer times can't be performed (1-3-4). Choosing cards means ≥1 — "any number" can't be 0 (1-3-6).
- **Turn player chooses first** on simultaneous choices (1-3-5).
- **Numbers:** modifiers always resolve to an integer (1-3-7), are applied to the original value and summed (1-3-8); a cost can never go below 0 (1-3-9). Shuffles must randomize (1-3-10).
- **Declaring utilization of a card (1-3-11, rewritten in 4.1/4.2):** "utilizing" = playing, digivolving, using, or linking. You may declare only if the card can be utilized and its cost **or alternate cost** can be paid (1-3-11-1). If an alternate cost depends on processing conditions they must be met *now* (1-3-11-2); if it depends on **optional** processing conditions ("by suspending 2 Digimon, reduce the cost by 4"), you may declare only if they can be processed, and you **must perform as much of that processing as needed to pay** (1-3-11-3). DigiXros/Assembly likewise: declarable only if the alternate cost is payable, counting immediate-type effects the declaration itself triggers, and you must place as many materials as needed to pay (1-3-11-4). A changed cost **is** an "alternate cost" (4-2-3-1); exactly the stated value is paid, never more or less (4-2-2).

## 1. Turn structure & phases (§6, glossary)

Turn order of phases (6-1-2): **Unsuspend → Draw → Breeding → Main**. The next phase doesn't start until all processing for the current one resolves (6-1-3).

- **Unsuspend (6-2):** turn player unsuspends all their cards on the field simultaneously (6-2-1). `[Start of Your Turn]` effects trigger/activate **before** unsuspending (6-2-1-1, 15-16-11). Cards needn't unsuspend in any order (6-2-1-3).
- **Draw (6-3):** draw 1 (6-3-1). **The first player skips their draw on turn 1 only** (6-3-1-1).
- **Breeding (6-4):** hatch a Digi-Egg (reveal top egg into the empty breeding area), move a Lv.3+ Digimon from breeding to battle area, or do nothing (6-4-1). Breeding-area cards can't be affected by effects (3-4-7-3), can't trigger/activate effects (3-4-7-4), can't be chosen (3-4-7-5), can't meet activation conditions (3-4-7-7) and **can't even be referenced** (3-4-7-8) — except by effects that explicitly specify or reference breeding areas. Only 1 may occupy it (4-18-3).
- **Main (6-5):** in any order, any number of times (when no unresolved processing): play a Digimon/Tamer, digivolve, use an Option, link a card, attack, activate an activation-type effect, or **pass** (6-5-1).

## 2. Memory gauge — the turn-end engine (§1-4-2, §4-1, §4-2, §6-1-4, §6-6, glossary)

One shared gauge, your side and opponent's side, 0 in the center, max 10 each (1-4-2). Paying a cost moves the marker toward/into the opponent's side by exactly the cost's value (4-2-2).

- **Turn end (6-1-4-1):** the turn ends once memory is **≥1 on the opponent's side** *and* all current-phase processing has resolved — then the turn ends with the current phase.
- **Postponement (6-6-4):** if memory moves back to **0 or more on your side** at the end of turn, the end is postponed and the current phase continues (your turn keeps going).
- **Pass (6-5-1-7-1):** declaring a pass immediately moves memory to the opponent's **3**, regardless of where it was.
- **`<Overflow>` (4-19, ACE rule):** when a card with `<Overflow>` moves from the field or from under a card to another area, the memory marker moves by the stated value (4-19-1) **immediately, even mid-processing** (4-19-2); never when the card moves *onto* the field or *under* a card (4-19-3, 4-19-4). Simultaneous instances: turn player resolves all of theirs one at a time, then the non-turn player (4-19-5).

## 3. Playing cards & alternate plays (§7, §1-3-11)

- **Play procedure (7-1-3):** declare + reveal → pay play cost → place on field. If the card can no longer be played, the reveal returns to origin (not "removal"), and **memory doesn't move** when a cost can't be paid (7-1-2-3, 9-1-8).
- A Digimon or Tamer **can't attack the turn it was placed on the field** (7-1-2-1; 4.0 reworded 3.6's "can't unsuspend the turn it was played" into the attack prohibition itself — overridden by `<Rush>`/`<Vortex>`/etc.). Digivolving a Digimon the turn it was played **removes** the "played this turn" state, so it can attack (8-2-2-1-7).
- **DigiXros (7-2):** play a Digimon with DigiXros requirements, placing the specified cards from hand/battle area under it, reducing the play cost per card placed. Declared right before paying cost (7-2-2-2); not mandatory (7-2-2-12); placing 0 = not performed (7-2-2-9); an "X-card DigiXros" needs exactly X placed (7-2-2-10). A battle-area card placed as material is considered to **leave the field at that timing** (7-2-2-7).
- **Assembly (7-3):** like DigiXros but materials come **from the trash** (7-3-1); exact specified count required (7-3-2-4).
- **DUAL cards (4-6, new in 4.0):** a card that is either a Digimon card or an Option card (4-6-1); when utilizing it you **declare** which information you use (4-6-2). On the field it is a Digimon and can't be referenced as an "Option card in the battle area" (4-6-3). Its Digimon information (traits and above) gives Digimon effects; its Option information (lower text) gives Option effects (4-6-5, 4-6-6, 2-3-11-5). **Arts Digivolve (4-20, 2-11):** instead of the post-use trash of the DUAL Option (pending processing, 9-1-5), one of your cards on the field **may** digivolve into it without paying the cost — overwrite processing (4-20-2, 18-1).

## 4. Digivolution, costs & inherited effects (§8, §2-3-5, §3-4, §15-3)

- **Standard (8-1):** reveal, stack the new Digimon on a card meeting one of its digivolution requirements, pay that requirement's digivolve cost, then **draw 1** (8-1-3-3). A card may have **multiple digivolution requirements** — the player chooses which to use (8-1-2-1, 2-3-5); this is the engine's "cost choice" prompt point. Digivolution may proceed even when a draw is impossible (no card drawn) (8-1-2-8).
- **Identity:** each card placed on the field is a separate Digimon/Tamer (3-4-4); stacking a card on top (or the top card leaving to expose a different card) means the permanent **changed into** the new card — it is **not** a different, newly-placed Digimon (3-4-5), and a card that becomes a bottom card isn't a new card (3-1-3-1-3). A digivolved Digimon carries over its orientation (8-1-2-3). Only 1 digivolution per Digimon at a time (8-1-2-5). Ignoring requirements (alt-digivolve) still digivolves (8-1-2-2).
- **DNA digivolution (8-2):** the stacked cards digivolve *as different Digimon or Tamers* and the result carries over none of their states (8-2-2-1): it digivolves unsuspended (8-2-2-1-1), all stacked cards are **new cards** (8-2-2-1-2), attack / attack-target status is lost (8-2-2-1-3, -4), chosen-for/applied effects end (8-2-2-1-5), `[X Per Turn]` counts reset (8-2-2-1-6); only by an effect that specifically performs DNA digivolution (8-2-2-4).
- **Burst Digivolve (8-3):** return a Tamer to play; the top card of the burst-digivolved stack is trashed at end of the turn it digivolved (pending processing) — unless there are no stacked cards or that card isn't a Digimon (8-3-2-1).
- **App Fusion (8-4):** place a specified link card from a Digimon on top to digivolve, drawing 1 (8-4-3-3).
- **Inherited effects (15-3):** an effect gained from a digivolution card; can't be activated by just one card (15-3-1); treated as a **Digimon effect** regardless of the source card's category (15-3-2); **gained inherited effects carry over, with their state, when another card is stacked on top** (15-3-3, new in 4.3); `"this card"` in an inherited effect refers to the digivolution card itself (15-3-4).
- **Link cards (4-9, §10):** plugged in sideways (4-9-1); not a stacked card (4-9-2, 10-1-2-1) and not a card on the field (4-9-4); max 1 link card per card, the excess being trashed on linking (4-9-5) or by rule check (17-1-3-2-5); trashed when the host becomes a new card (4-9-6). The Digimon gains the link DP and link effects (4-3-4, 4-3-5).

## 5. Attacking, blocking, security checks, battles (§11–§14)

- **Attack timings (11-1-3):** Attack declaration → **Counter timing** → **Block timing** → Confirming success → End of attack. Each completes before the next begins (11-1-4). Only the turn player attacks (11-1-2); 1 Digimon = 1 attack (11-2-3).
- **Targets (11-2-7-1):** choose the opponent **player** or **1 of their suspended Digimon**. If the target Digimon is later removed, the target stays "removed" and the **attack fails** (11-2-6). Target can switch by rule/effect (e.g. `<Raid>`, `<Blocker>`) but not to an existing target (11-2-7-3).
- **Counter (11-3):** the timing when the *attacked* non-turn player's `[Counter]` effect can activate (11-3-1; `[Counter]` is a special activation-type timing, 15-16-14-1); only one `[Counter]` per attack (11-3-2). **Block (11-4 / §12):** the attacked non-turn player may use a `<Blocker>` Digimon (11-4-1); the target switches to the blocker; **only 1 block per attack** (12-1-2); a block can't be made by a Digimon that can't suspend (12-1-4), nor by the attack target itself (12-1-5).
- **Confirming success (11-5):** attack on player with **≥1 security** → perform a security check (11-5-1-1); on player with **0 security** → attacker **wins** unless it can't perform security checks (11-5-1-2-1); on a Digimon → a **battle** (11-5-1-3-1). **An unsuccessful attack still reaches end-of-attack timing** — nothing else happens, but `[End of Attack]` timing occurs (11-5-1-4, 11-5-1-4-1; new in 4.0).
- **Security checks (13):** **only 1 security check per attack**; `<Security A. +x>` modifies the number of cards checked *inside* that one check (13-1-2, new in 3.7/4.0). The check is **mandatory** (13-1-4), one card at a time (13-1-3). If the checking Digimon is no longer in the battle area it can't check further (13-1-5). A checked card leaves the stack and is "in no area" (13-1-6); a checked **Digimon** becomes a **Security Digimon** (13-1-7) — not a regular Digimon, only its security effects work, and effects targeting regular Digimon can't hit it (glossary). Multi-checks flip one at a time, fully resolving each — triggered effects (13-1-8-2), then the security battle (13-1-8-3), then trash the revealed card unless it belongs to an area (13-1-8-4) — before the next (glossary).
- **Battles (14):** compare DP; lower **loses and is deleted** (14-2-1, 14-2-2); equal DP → **both lose** (14-2-1-3). **Security Digimon are not deleted when they lose** (14-2-3).

## 6. Effect rules — categories, timing, processing order (§15)

**The single most error-prone area for faithful implementation. Read this before scripting any non-trivial card.**

- **Processing order inside one effect (15-1-2):** text order — but **any optional processing conditions affecting the effect are decided *before* the effect is processed** (added in 4.3).
- **Effect categories (15-8-1):**
  - **Persistent (15-8-2):** always active, no trigger (e.g. `[Your Turn] +1000 DP`). Active as soon as conditions are met (15-8-2-2); off when no longer met (15-8-2-3). Multiple overlap; conflicting → **later-activated wins, except prohibiting effects** (15-8-2-5). Persistent-with-processing-conditions are active only while those conditions hold (15-8-2-6).
  - **Trigger-type (15-8-3):** triggers when conditions met, then activates (`[When Attacking]`, `[On Deletion]`, …). **Can't activate during rule/effect processing** (15-8-3-2) — it waits as *pending activation*. Once triggered it stays triggered even if memory changes (15-8-3-6). References during processing use the **state at trigger time** for removal/"when X is played" cases (15-8-3-8). A self-deletion/self-trash trigger fires in the area where the card was, then becomes pending activation once the card is in the trash (4-15-2, 4-16-2).
  - **Activation-type (15-8-4):** player-activated `[Main]` effects, optional; declarable only while processing/optional conditions can be met (15-8-4-3-1, 15-8-4-4-1).
  - **Immediate-type (15-8-5):** "when X **would**" effects that **interrupt right before their cause** (15-8-5-2) — e.g. `<Decoy>`, `<Armor Purge>`, `<Evade>`, `<Barrier>`, `<Guard>`, `<Detach>`, would-be-deleted/removed/digivolve. Only simultaneous with other immediate-type (15-8-5-3); each can activate **once** until the interrupting cause resolves (15-8-5-4).
- **Processing order for simultaneous triggers (15-4-3):** triggers at the same timing are **pending activation**, resolved **1 at a time** (15-4-2-3); the **turn player chooses and activates all of theirs first**, then the non-turn player chooses theirs (15-4-3-5-1, 15-4-3-5-2). Rule-check-induced triggers join the same timing (15-4-3-3). **Derived triggering** (a new trigger arising while simultaneous ones resolve) activates **before** the still-pending ones (15-4-5-2/3). An activated effect is resolved in full even if its card becomes a new card mid-processing (15-4-1-2).
- **Trigger vs processing conditions:**
  - **Trigger conditions (15-5):** the "when …" that fires the effect; a single condition triggers **once** even if met multiple times simultaneously (15-5-2; with "each"/"every" text the count can still be referenced, 4-27-4).
  - **Processing conditions (15-6):** "if"/"while" gates on a process; an effect can't activate if **none** of its processing conditions are met (15-6-3); different processes in one effect are gated independently (15-6-2).
  - **Optional processing conditions (15-7):** "**By** X, Y" — paying X is the player's choice (15-7-1; optional processing, 15-9-2-2); if X isn't executed, Y can't be (15-7-2); you **can't perform only part** of X (15-7-3); **4.3 §15-7-4 (REVERSED from 3.6): if X's contents can't be executed, the player *can't choose* to execute X** — an unpayable cost is not offered (3.6 let the player "choose" it and then fizzle); you *may* choose X even if Y can't be executed (15-7-5). A mandatory effect that *invokes* another effect doesn't make that effect's optional condition mandatory (15-15-7-4).
- **Mandatory vs optional processing (15-9):** mandatory text **must** be executed — the player can't decline (15-9-1-2) — and is performed **whenever possible** (15-1-5). Optional text the player chooses (15-9-2). **(No-approximations corollary: never auto-resolve an optional choice or the cost half of a "by X, Y" — surface it to the action space; and per 15-7-4 never offer a cost that can't be paid; see rule 17.)**
- **Prohibition beats permission:** a prohibiting effect takes precedence over an enabling one (15-1-3).
- **Targets (15-10/15-11):** "X Digimon"/"X cards" choose individual targets (15-10-2); "all" doesn't choose — it's **overall processing** that also catches later-added matches (15-11-2-2). Individual processing locks onto its target even if it later stops meeting the condition (15-11-1-3-2); overall processing tracks the live set (15-11-2-3).
- **Add vs change information (15-12):** "treated as …" adds info, overwriting same-kind info, one of {play cost, level, DP} at a time (15-12-1-3); "change the name/…" changes info but can't add info a card never had (15-12-2-2). **A persistent effect's added/changed information always wins while it is active and reverts when it is lost** (15-12-1-3, 15-12-2-3; 4.1).
- **Effects that reveal cards (15-15-3, merged "Reveal" + "Search/Look at" in 4.0 — flagged in the manual):** a revealed/looked-at card does **not** by itself change its area's card count (so "removed from your security stack" doesn't trigger) but can't be referenced as a card in that area while revealed (15-15-3-1); **if the revealed/looked-at cards are then trashed/added to hand**, the area count *is* modified and those removal effects **do** trigger (15-15-3-2). Nothing can add cards to an area while all of it is revealed (15-15-3-3); the whole reveal is one process until every card is placed (15-15-3-4); a draw during a deck reveal comes from the *unrevealed* cards (15-15-3-5). Searching/looking at reveals only to the specified player (15-15-3-9-1), the acting player by default — hand searches are visible to both (15-15-3-9-2); returned cards go back per the area's rules and any follow-up processing happens after the return (15-15-3-9-3). A classic interaction trap.
- **Effect icons (15-14):** `[X Per Turn]` counts each activation toward X (15-14-1-1), even if the processing can't be executed (15-14-1-4); the count **resets** when the turn changes or when the card becomes a new card (e.g. via DNA digivolution) (15-14-1-5). `{Hand}` activates when revealed from hand (15-14-2); `{Trash}` from trash (15-14-3); `{Breeding}` from the breeding area (15-14-4); `{Security}` while face-up in the security stack during a check (15-14-5).
- **Gained effects (15-13):** carry over the effect's state at the time it was gained, even if the source card is buried/removed (15-13-2); a **gained `[On Deletion]` stays pending even after the card is in the trash** (15-13-3, 4.3).
- **Borrowed effects (15-15-7):** "activate another effect": when several could activate, choose one, process it, then choose the next (15-15-7-2); "activate as this card's effect" attributes it to the activating card (15-15-7-3); the invoked effect's optional conditions stay optional (15-15-7-4).
- **Card-information references (2-3-3, 4.3):** "a card with `<Blocker>`" references only cards that **always** possess the keyword, not ones granted it (2-3-3-3); "a keyword effect that executes processing" excludes conditional ("if"/"while"/"by") forms (2-3-3-4). `(Rule)` lines grant traits / names / card numbers / deck-count limits (2-3-4-2 … 2-3-4-6).
- **"Also" (4-29, 4.3):** "they also gain X" shares the preceding processing's **duration and targets**. **"Each"/"Every" (4-27):** for-each stack targeting counts from the top/bottom (4-27-2); "process A and process B for each X" does all of A then all of B (4-27-3).

## 7. Common interaction gotchas (each cited)

- **`[Security]` effects jump the queue:** they activate immediately **without** pending activation, so they take activation precedence even when triggering simultaneously with other effects (15-16-10-2).
- **0-DP deletion is a rule check, not a battle:** rule checks run simultaneously for every listed target (17-1-3): a battle-area Digimon at 0 DP is deleted (17-1-3-1-1); a Digimon with no DP, an Option in the battle area, a non-Digimon in the breeding area, a face-down field card, excess / unqualified / wrong-category link cards are trashed (17-1-3-2-1 … -7). Rule checks don't run during rule/effect processing (17-1-2-1, 17-1-2-2) — a Digimon at 0 DP mid-processing isn't deleted until the current processing resolves. *(Matches engine memory `project_dp_zero_deletion`.)*
- **Pending processing** (e.g. "lose 3 memory at end of turn", `<Execute>`/`<Piercing>` tails, Burst Digivolve trash) resolves at its predetermined timing like a triggered effect (18-1-1) and joins simultaneous triggers (18-1-2). A used Option is trashed **immediately** as pending processing once its first `[Main]` resolves; nothing interrupts the gap (9-1-5, 4.0).
- **`<Piercing>` ordering:** on a battle that deletes a Digimon with `[On Deletion]`, the `[On Deletion]` resolves **first**, then `<Piercing>` is processed immediately before end-of-attack timing (16-7-4); the security check from `<Piercing>` is **mandatory** (16-7-3); only 1 security check per attack even if `<Piercing>` activates several times (16-7-5, 13-1-2); no check if no Digimon remains to perform it (16-7-6) or the player has 0 security (11-5-1-1).
- **`<Blocker>` cap:** even multiple `<Blocker>` instances allow only 1 block in a block timing (16-5-3, 12-1-2).
- **Keyword multiplicity (changed in 4.0):** the per-keyword "multiple instances overlap when they trigger" rules are **gone**; only `<Security A.>` (16-4-3), `<Blocker>` (16-5-3), `<Reboot>` (16-11-3), `<Link +>` (16-40-3) and `<Piercing>` (16-7-5) say anything about multiples. Everything else falls back to 15-5-2 / 15-4-3.
- **`<Collision>` forces blocks** and grants the opponent's Digimon `<Blocker>` while the carrier attacks (16-30-1) — "all opponent Digimon gain Blocker" affects Digimon; "forced to block" affects the opponent player (16-30-4).
- **`<Jamming>`** stops deletion only against **Security Digimon**, but its `<Security A. +x>` extra check still happens (glossary `<Jamming>`).
- **Removed-then-target:** if the attack target Digimon is removed mid-attack, the target stays removed and the attack fails — it does **not** redirect to the player (11-2-6) — but end-of-attack timing still occurs (11-5-1-4-1).
- **Suspended is targetable:** only **suspended** opponent Digimon can be chosen as an attack target (11-2-7-1); unsuspended Digimon can only be hit via effects (e.g. `<Raid>`, `<Vortex>`, `<Execute>`).
- **Tokens:** a token leaving the field is removed from the game but *is* considered to have been placed in that area (4-21-5, 4.0 wording); cards can't be stacked on tokens (4-21-3).
- **"Option card in the battle area"** means only an Option *placed there by an effect* (4-28-1); a field DUAL card is never one (4-6-3).

## 8. Keywords added since 3.6 (§16-42 … §16-47 — see `keyword-semantics.md` for the full table)

- `<Use Req.>` (16-42, 4.0): persistent; ignore Option colour requirements with the specified Digimon/Tamers on the field.
- `<Ascension>` (16-43, 4.0): trigger on this Digimon's deletion; **may** place this card on top of the security stack.
- `<Engage>` (16-44, 4.1): `[End of Your Turn]` trigger; this Digimon **may** attack.
- `<Guard>` (16-45, 4.1): immediate; when another of your Digimon would leave the battle area by an opponent's effect, **by deleting this Digimon** it doesn't leave.
- `<Detach (…)>` (16-46, 4.2): immediate; when this would leave the battle area other than by your effects, **by trashing 1 of its specified link cards** it doesn't leave.
- `<Succession (…)>` (16-47, 4.2): persistent; gains all effects other than `<Succession>` on its topmost specified digivolution card.
